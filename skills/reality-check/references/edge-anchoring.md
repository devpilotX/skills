# Edge anchoring

Phase 4. This carries the anti-generic guarantee.

## The principle

Consensus suppression on its own produces different answers. It does not produce answers that are
right for this person. Uniqueness has to come from the inputs, because the model has no private
knowledge, and this user does.

So differentiation gets derived from facts only this user possesses. If the output is anchored in
their actual position, it cannot be the answer a hundred thousand other people received, because they
are not in that position. It also makes the advice harder to copy, since a competitor would have to
acquire the same assets first.

## Inventory

Ask only what the situation needs. At most three questions at a time, picked for the power to change
the verdict. Never dump the whole list on someone.

Position. Where do they physically live, and which supplier and labour markets does that reach
cheaply? Which languages do they operate in natively? Which legal jurisdiction, and what does it
allow or forbid?

Credentials and access. Licences, certifications, registrations. Who already takes their call: family
businesses, former employers, industry contacts. Any existing audience, customer list, or
distribution.

Resources. Capital available, and how much of it can actually be risked. Runway in months at current
burn. Dependents. Assets already owned, including land, vehicles, equipment, premises, inventory, and
code.

Hard-won knowledge. Where have they worked, especially in unglamorous operational roles? A year in a
logistics back office beats any market report. What do they know about how an industry really works
that outsiders get wrong? What have they already failed at, since earned negative knowledge is an
asset.

Tolerance. What can they stand that most people cannot: night shifts, cold calling, physical labour,
regulatory paperwork, angry customers, slow payback, working alone. What will they genuinely not do,
because a plan built on work they refuse is already dead.

## Grading rule

An edge counts only if it is non-transferable, meaning hard for a competitor to acquire quickly.

"I'm passionate about it" is not an edge. "My uncle runs the regional distributor" might be.

"I work hard" is not an edge. "I hold a commercial driving licence and own my truck outright" might be.

"I know how to code" is not an edge. "I spent four years maintaining billing systems for clinics"
might be.

"I have fifty thousand in savings" is weak on its own. "I have fifty thousand and a building I own
rent free" is different.

"I'm a fast learner" is not an edge. "I negotiate natively in the language of a supplier market that
most buyers cannot" might be.

The left side of each pair is what everyone claims. The right side takes years or money to match.

Tolerance is the most underrated edge. Willingness to do what others find tedious or degrading is a
real moat, and it appears in no market report.

## Routing

For each surviving candidate from phase 3, answer four questions.

Which specific inventory item does this exploit?

Could someone without that item execute this just as well? If yes, the candidate is not anchored.
Find the real anchor or cut it.

What does the edge change quantitatively: cost, access, speed, price tolerance, or the payback period
they can survive?

How long until the edge decays? An edge with a six month half-life does not support a three year plan.

## The swap test

Mandatory gate before any recommendation ships. Substitute a different person: different city,
different budget, different background, different network. Re-read the recommendation.

If it still reads as sensible, it is generic. Cut it and return to phase 3.

If it is now clearly wrong or impossible, it is anchored. Ship it.

Advice that is good for everyone is tied to no one's situation, and that is exactly the output being
eliminated.

## When there is no edge

Sometimes the honest finding is that the user has no relevant advantage in the space they picked. Say
it directly and give the two real options.

Acquire an edge first. Name the specific job, skill, licence, relationship, or capital, and roughly
how long it takes.

Change targets. Apply the edges they do have to a different problem, even if it is less exciting than
the original idea.

"You have no advantage here, and here are two paths to one" beats a plan that quietly depends on an
advantage they lack.
