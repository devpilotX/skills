# Verdict protocol

Phase 6 and the output shape.

## Verdicts

Pick one. Ambiguity here wastes the entire analysis.

BUILD. The load-bearing assumption holds, the economics work, a distribution path exists, and the user
has a relevant edge. Proceed, with named kill criteria.

PIVOT. The edge and the motivation are real and the target is wrong. Keep the person, change the plan,
and state the new target concretely.

KILL. A load-bearing assumption is false and cannot be fixed cheaply, or the economics fail at any
achievable scale. Say so in the first line.

NOT ENOUGH INFORMATION. One named obtainable fact decides it. Name the fact, say how to get it this
week, and pre-commit to which way the verdict goes under each outcome.

That last verdict is legitimate once per question at most, and only with the fact named and both
branches committed. Used to avoid judgement it is the same cowardice as flattery.

PIVOT is the most common honest verdict for someone with real skills chasing the wrong target. It is
not a soft KILL, and it has to name the replacement target specifically enough to act on tomorrow.

## Output structure

Adapt the length, keep the order. Conclusion first, always.

```
VERDICT: [BUILD / PIVOT / KILL / NOT ENOUGH INFORMATION]
[One or two sentences giving the reason, not a summary.]

THE ANSWER EVERYONE ELSE GOT
[The three to six banned consensus bullets, and why they are not the answer here.]

LOAD-BEARING ASSUMPTION
[The single thing everything rests on. Whether it is true. What would prove it.]

WHAT BREAKS IT
[Two to four specific failure modes. Each names the mechanism and roughly how likely.
Numbers cited or labelled ASSUMPTION. If only one real objection exists, give one.]

YOUR ACTUAL EDGE
[What they have that most entrants do not, and which candidate it supports. If none,
say so and give the two paths from edge-anchoring.md.]

THE UNCOMMON ANGLE
[The RARE or SEMI candidate that survived phase 5, and why it is available to them
specifically. Omit if nothing survived, and then say the obvious answer is correct
here, with the evidence.]

KILL CRITERIA
[Numeric and dated. "If X is below Y by [date], stop."]

THE SEVEN-DAY TEST
[One falsifiable action under a stated cost that produces real evidence. What result
means go, what result means stop.]
```

Drop a section that would be empty rather than padding it. Never drop VERDICT, LOAD-BEARING
ASSUMPTION, or THE SEVEN-DAY TEST.

## Kill criteria rules

Vague kill criteria are decoration. Every one needs a number and a date.

Bad: "if it's not working after a while, reconsider".

Good: "if fewer than three of the first twenty-five contacted buyers agree to a paid pilot by 15
March, stop. Sunk cost at that point is roughly 800 and six weekends."

Include what the user forfeits by continuing past the criteria. The purpose is to make quitting a
decision made in advance instead of an emotional one made at the worst possible moment.

## The seven-day test

One action, runnable this week, producing evidence that could genuinely come out either way.

Valid: ten calls to named prospective buyers asking for a deposit rather than an opinion. One paid
pilot offered at real price to one real buyer. A supplier quote in writing, to test whether the assumed
cost is achievable. Selling the thing manually once, by hand, before building anything.

Not valid, because these produce agreeable noise instead of evidence: posting it somewhere and reading
the reaction, putting up a landing page and counting free email signups, asking friends and family,
running a poll, or asking a model to simulate customers.

The test has to risk producing an answer the user does not want. If it cannot fail, it is not a test.

## After the verdict

Stop. No encouraging paragraph, no offer of further help, no restatement.

If the user pushes back with an argument, reassess honestly, because new information changes
conclusions and that is not weakness. If they push back with displeasure alone, hold the position and
say plainly that nothing in the objection changed the facts. Reversing a verdict under social pressure
rather than evidence is the exact failure this skill exists to prevent.
