# Running a price change

Changing a live price is a trust operation as much as a revenue one. The mechanics below protect the
revenue without breaking the base.

## Before you change anything

Model the outcome, not just the headline. A 20 percent increase does not yield 20 percent more revenue,
because some customers leave. Estimate the churn the increase causes at each segment, subtract the lost
revenue, and confirm the net still rises. If a modest churn rate wipes out the gain, the increase is
priced wrong or aimed at the wrong segment.

Segment the impact. A new prospect, a customer on an old plan, and a large contract renewal each
experience the change differently. Model them separately, because a blanket change that suits new
buyers can drive away the customers who trusted you earliest.

Decide the floor and the ceiling. The floor is your cost, below which you lose money on every sale. The
ceiling is the buyer's alternative. A change that pushes past the ceiling sends buyers to the
alternative regardless of how you communicate it.

## Grandfathering

Grandfathering means existing customers keep their current price, permanently or for a defined period,
while new customers pay the new price. It buys goodwill and removes the churn risk from the base at the
cost of slower revenue capture.

Full grandfather. Existing customers never move. Cleanest for trust, slowest for revenue. Fits when the
base is small and loyal and the growth is in new customers.

Time boxed grandfather. Existing customers keep the old price for a set number of billing cycles, then
move with notice. Balances trust and capture. Fits most situations.

No grandfather with notice. Everyone moves, but with real warning measured in billing cycles. Only
defensible when the increase is small and clearly justified, such as a cost passthrough, and always
paired with an easy way to leave.

## The communication plan

Write the message before you touch the price. It answers four questions plainly: what is changing, why,
when it takes effect, and what it means for this specific customer.

Give notice in billing cycles, not days. A monthly customer needs at least one full cycle. An annual
customer needs notice before their renewal, not after.

Tie the increase to added value where it is real. A price rise alongside genuine new capability is far
easier to accept than a bare rise. Do not invent value that is not there, which the buyer sees through.

Give a path to stay. An annual prepay at the old rate, a lock in window, or a downgrade option lets a
price sensitive customer choose to stay rather than churn on principle.

Segment the send. The customer who has paid for three years hears a different message from the prospect
who signed up last week. A single blast to both reads as careless to the loyal one.

## Discount discipline

Every discount teaches. A buyer who gets 20 percent off learns the real price is lower, tells peers, and
the next buyer opens by asking for the same. Undisciplined discounting resets the whole price downward
and the list price becomes fiction.

Set the rules in advance. A maximum discount, an approver for each level, and a reason required to grant
one. A discount nobody has to justify is a discount everybody gets.

Trade the discount for something. A longer term, an annual prepay, a case study, a reference call, a
faster close. A discount given for nothing trains the buyer that price is negotiable by default.

Prefer time boxed or exchange based cuts over standing ones. A launch discount that ends is a price. A
standing discount that never ends is just a lower price you are pretending is temporary, and it removes
your room to run a real promotion later.

Watch the discount rate as a metric. If the average selling price drifts well below list, the list
price is wrong and the discount is doing the real pricing. Fix the list rather than discounting to it
every time.

## Second order effects to watch

Support load. A higher price raises expectations, so tickets get more demanding and slower to satisfy.
A lower price attracts customers who file more tickets per dollar. Staff for the mix the price creates.

Churn timing. Price sensitive customers acquired on a discount churn faster, so a cheaper price can
lower lifetime value even as it lifts signups. Track churn by acquisition price.

Sales cycle. A higher price often lengthens the cycle and pulls in more stakeholders on the buyer side.
Budget for the longer close rather than assuming the old velocity holds.

Customer mix. Price is a filter. It selects who you get, and the customers a price attracts shape the
product roadmap, the support burden, and the brand. A change to price is a change to who your customers
are.
