# Genre playbooks

This file quotes phrasings it tells you to avoid, so it declares linter exemptions above.

One set of rules applied to every document produces something as artificial as the thing it was meant to
fix. A changelog is a list because a changelog is a list. These are the per genre adjustments, and where
they conflict with the main file, the genre wins.

## Reference and encyclopedic

Strictest genre. No promotional register, no evaluative adjectives, no section that exists to summarise
the article. Every claim carries a source, and the source is checked against the sentence it supports.

Lead with what the subject is, in a plain copula, then the facts in an order the material dictates. No
paragraph about how the subject matters.

Ban the awards section that lists two minor prizes, the future prospects section, and any closing
paragraph that starts by conceding difficulties and ends on vague optimism.

## Documentation and README

Lists, tables and code fences are correct here and should not be converted to prose. A command belongs
in a fence, and a set of options belongs in a table.

What still applies: no promotional opening, no feature list written as marketing, and no claim about
speed or reliability without a number or a link.

Lead with what it does and how to run it. Install before explanation. The reader arrived with a task.

Say what it does not do and what it will not support. A README that admits nothing is a sales page.

## Blog post and essay

The genre with the most room and the most temptation. Contractions, first person, sentence fragments and
a strong opinion all belong.

Open mid thought or with a specific incident. The warm up paragraph that names the topic and promises to
explore it is the single clearest sign nobody edited this.

Earn every section break. If you cannot say what changed between two sections, they are one section.

End on the last thing you have to say, then stop. No restatement, no call to reflect.

## Email and message

Shortest useful form. Say the ask in the first sentence, then the context. The reader is deciding
whether to keep reading in about two seconds.

No pleasantry that carries no information. Drop the sentence hoping the reader is well, the sentence
saying you wanted to reach out, and any closing that invites them to let you know if they have
questions, unless you have a specific question in mind.

One ask per message. Two asks produce one answer.

## Commit message and pull request description

Imperative mood in the subject, under about seventy characters, no trailing full stop. Say what changed,
and why when the why is not obvious from the diff.

The body explains the reason, the alternative rejected, and the risk. It does not restate the diff,
which the reader can already see.

No attribution trailer naming a tool or a model. It dates the commit, tells a reviewer nothing about the
change, and is the first thing stripped.

For a pull request: what changed, what you tested, and what you did not cover. A description claiming
completeness invites a reviewer to find the gap.

## Marketing and landing copy

Claims here must be checkable because a buyer will check them. A specific number beats a superlative,
and a named customer beats an adjective.

Banned outright: unparalleled, revolutionary, game changing, industry leading, and any statistic without
a source. Also banned: invented urgency, fake scarcity and a countdown that resets.

Say who it is not for. That single move does more for credibility than any amount of praise.

## Biography and profile

Plain copulas, dates, places, roles. What the person did, in order, with the thing they are known for
placed where a reader would look for it.

No sentence about their passion, their journey, or the mark they left. If the achievement needs an
adjective to sound impressive, it is not the achievement to lead with.

Do not assert that information is private or undocumented as a way of filling a gap. Leave the gap.

## Social post

Length is the constraint that does the work. One idea, no preamble, no thread announcing a thread.

No hashtag stack, no emoji as bullet points, no sentence that exists to bait a reply.

## Changelog and release notes

A list, grouped by kind of change, newest first. Each entry says what changed from the reader's side,
not which function was edited.

Name breaking changes first and give the migration step. A release note that hides the breaking change
under a feature list costs someone a weekend.

State the version, the date, and what you did not fix.

## Fiction

The defaults collapse hard here, so watch for them specifically: a protagonist named Elara, whispering
woods, the smell of ozone, a dog barking in the distance, a character who is a mix of two traits, and
anyone releasing a breath they did not know they were holding.

Concrete sensory detail that only fits this scene is the cure, the same as everywhere else. A room with
a specific broken thing in it beats a room described as cluttered.

Dialogue carries the register. If every character speaks in the same balanced cadence, none of them is a
person yet.
