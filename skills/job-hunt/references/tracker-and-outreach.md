# Tracking the funnel and reaching a human

A job search is a funnel, and a funnel is only manageable if it is measured. This file holds the tracker
layout, the ratios that locate the broken stage, and the outreach templates that get an application in
front of a person instead of a portal. Fill the templates with the user's own facts; the bracketed parts
are fields to complete, not text to send as written.

## The tracker

One row per employer, these columns:

  Employer, the company name.
  Route, the specific human path in: a named person, a mutual connection, a community, a former colleague.
  Role, the exact title and level applied for.
  Date contacted, when the first message or application went out.
  Stage, one of: researched, contacted, responded, interviewing, final, offer, closed.
  Next action, the single next thing to do and when.
  Notes, anything learned that changes the approach.

Review the table weekly. Recompute the funnel ratios monthly so a trend is visible rather than a single
noisy week.

## The ratios that locate the problem

From the last thirty days, count applications sent, first responses, first interviews, final stages, and
offers. Then read the drop-offs:

  Many applications, almost no responses: targeting or the written application is wrong, or the channel
  has no human at the end. Volume is not the fix here, and this is the most common case.
  Responses but no first interview: the CV opens well and does not survive scrutiny, usually because it
  claims responsibilities without evidence of outcomes.
  Interviews but no final stages: the competency or technical answers are not landing, or the preparation
  is generic. Ask for feedback, which is sometimes given at this point.
  Final stages but no offers: competition at the last step, a reference problem, or a late-surfacing
  mismatch. Ask explicitly for feedback.
  Offers but poor ones: the target is too low, or the negotiation is being skipped.

Change one thing at a time so any improvement has an identifiable cause. If a stage has not moved in three
weeks, the approach at that stage is the thing to change.

## Finding the route to a human

An application with an internal referral is processed differently from one without, which is the single
largest lever in the process. For each shortlisted employer, look for:

  A former colleague or classmate who works there now.
  A mutual connection who would make an introduction.
  A person who wrote publicly about the team or the problem.
  A community, meetup, or event where the team shows up.

Record the route next to the name. A shortlist with no routes is a list of hopes.

## Outreach message, a mutual connection

Keep it short enough to read on a phone.

  Hi [name], we both know [connection]. I am moving toward [role] and [company] came up because [specific
  reason tied to what they do]. I spent [duration] doing [relevant work] and shipped [one piece of
  evidence]. Would you be open to a fifteen minute call, or could you point me to the right person? No
  problem either way.

## Outreach message, a cold contact at the company

  Hi [name], I saw [specific thing they built, wrote, or shipped]. I am applying for [role] and wanted to
  ask one question rather than only sending an application into the portal: [a real, specific question
  about the work]. For context, I did [relevant work] for [duration]. Happy to keep it to email if that
  is easier.

## Referral request to someone who already knows your work

  Hi [name], [company] is hiring for [role] and it fits what I have been doing. You have seen my work on
  [specific project]. Would you be comfortable referring me, or introducing me to whoever owns the role? I
  can send a short summary you can forward so it costs you almost no time.

## What not to do

Do not send the same message to twenty people with the name swapped. The specific reason is the whole
point, and its absence is obvious. Do not follow up more than twice. Do not ask for a job in the first
message to a stranger; ask for a conversation or a pointer, which is a smaller thing to grant.
